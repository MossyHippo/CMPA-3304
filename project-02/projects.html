AmiGo – Music for All

"What if every child had a patient music teacher, no matter where they live or what their family can afford?"

The problem

Music education is out of reach for many children because of cost, distance, or unreliable power and internet.

Who it's for

Children who have been left out of music education, and the parents and teachers who support them.

What I did

  • Designed an AI tutor that watches posture and listens to pitch and timing through a phone or tablet.
  • Built in privacy by design: parental controls, data deletion, and opt-in data sharing.
  • Planned for offline mode and solar charging, under a nonprofit model.
  • Co-designed with musicians, educators, child-development experts, and parents, grounded in UNESCO AI principles.

The Bakery

"If your personal data is the secret recipe, why are we handing it out for free?"

The problem

Trackers collect our data invisibly, and cookie banners push us to click "Accept All" before we understand what we're agreeing to.

Who it's for

Everyday web users who want more control over their data without needing to learn the jargon.

What I did

  • Framed the opportunity with five "How might we" statements, starting with making invisible trackers visible.
  • Designed a browser extension where you are the baker and cookies become customers: Regulars, Loiterers, and Recipe Thieves who resell your data.
  • Gave every site an at-a-glance risk badge, and rewarded privacy actions such as sending a Do Not Track request.
  • Built four low-fidelity wireframes and a product design proposal.

Magic: The Accessible

"What would a frustration-free game look like for neurodivergent players, including me?"

The problem

Magic: The Gathering asks players to track a complex board state and a chain of triggered abilities. That creates a heavy cognitive burden, especially for players with diagnosed or undiagnosed learning and attention differences.

Who it's for

New learners, kitchen-table and online players, and the people they play with, while respecting tournament rules.

What I did

  • Mapped common attention challenges to what they look like at the game table, from the player's view and from the table's.
  • Prototyped an Ability Tracker that lines up triggers in order, flags optional "may" choices, and does the board math.
  • Added a "UI loudness" slider so each player decides how much help they get.
  • Designed a within-subjects study and a three-phase questionnaire to measure cognitive load and player agency.
